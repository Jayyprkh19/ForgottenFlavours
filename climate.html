Climate Change

Understanding the biggest threat to our planet's biodiversity

How Climate Change Affects Nature

Climate change is not a distant threat—it's happening now, and it's impacting species and ecosystems worldwide. Rising temperatures, unpredictable weather patterns, and sea-level rise are affecting food sources, habitats, and reproduction cycles of many living organisms.

🌍 Key Factors: Greenhouse gas emissions (especially CO₂ and methane), deforestation, industrial pollution, melting glaciers, and ocean acidification are all major contributors. The last decade was the hottest on record globally.

⚠️ Threats to Biodiversity: From coral bleaching in oceans to vanishing glaciers in the Arctic, the chain reaction of climate disruption is threatening countless species. Animals such as polar bears and snow leopards, and plants like wild spices and herbs, are already at risk. Warmer oceans are also leading to increased marine heatwaves, affecting aquatic species reproduction and survival.

📉 Facts: According to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), more than 1 million species are currently threatened with extinction due to climate-related factors. Coral reefs could decline by 70–90% if global warming exceeds 1.5°C.

🔮 The Future: If we do not take strong climate action, extreme heatwaves, loss of biodiversity, food insecurity, and rising sea levels will become more common. But with awareness, sustainable choices, and global cooperation, we still have time to change the course.

Protecting nature is not optional—it’s essential for our own survival. Forgotten species, flavors, and ecosystems all depend on the actions we take today.
